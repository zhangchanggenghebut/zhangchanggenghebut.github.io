On 3 November 2026 in Honolulu, Hawaii, Prof. Changgeng Zhang of Hebei University of Technology chaired the poster session on *Emerging Magnetic Sensors and Functional Electromagnetic Systems* at the invitation of the organisers of MMM 2026. The following afternoon he presented two research posters, one on predicting hysteresis in electrical steel and the other on computing the magnetic field produced by a coil with a nonlinear magnetic core.

## Chairing the magnetic sensors session

Zhang chaired Session AQ from 9:30 a.m. to 12:30 p.m. local time on 3 November. The posters illustrated different ways to detect weak magnetic signals. One study used an optically pumped magnetometer to detect magnetic nanoparticles. Another investigated whether concentrating magnetic flux at a giant magnetoresistive biosensor could improve its sensitivity.

## Two posters on magnetic field computation and hysteresis

On 4 November, from 3 to 6 p.m. in the Coral 4 poster hall, Zhang presented two studies on behalf of the team.

**Calculating the field in a practical coil (DS-05).** In this study, first-authored by Rawal Shahbaz, the team trained a gradient-enhanced physics-informed neural network to solve a nonlinear magnetostatic problem. The model incorporates the field equations, the nonlinear magnetic behaviour of the core and the conditions at material interfaces. Zhang used flux-density and error maps to show how the team checked the predicted field across the coil, air and core.

**Predicting hysteresis in grain-oriented electrical steel (DS-06).** In work first-authored by Sujun Babu, the team modified the reversible field in Hauser’s energy-based model so that it varies with magnetisation. Zhang showed how the team used hysteresis loops measured at 50 Hz, with peak flux densities from 0.1 to 1.5 T, to identify model parameters and check predictions of loop shape and magnetic loss.
