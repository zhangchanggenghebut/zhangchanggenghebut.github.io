On 21 May 2025, Haitao Zhang and Guochen Niu successfully defended their master’s theses at the School of Electrical Engineering, Hebei University of Technology. Both worked with Prof. Changgeng Zhang on soft magnetic cores for electrical equipment. Haitao measured how a lamination changes length during a magnetisation cycle. Guochen measured how much more power a core dissipates when its laminations are pressed together.

## Haitao Zhang measures how a lamination changes length

For his thesis on magnetostriction in nanocrystalline alloys, Haitao combined a double-yoke magnetic tester with an L-shaped optical path for laser vibrometry. The setup recorded magnetisation and strain at the same time. He tested the laminations under sinusoidal and rectangular excitation, as well as waveforms containing harmonics, and used a sequence-to-sequence model to predict strain over a cycle.

In tests at 5 and 15 kHz, Haitao progressively shortened the voltage pulse within each cycle. As the duty cycle fell from 1.0 to 0.2, the rounded wings of the butterfly-shaped strain loops became visibly truncated. The loops also showed greater asymmetry at 15 kHz. ## Guochen Niu measures the cost of compressing the core

For his thesis on how stress affects nanocrystalline and amorphous alloys, Guochen modified a magnetic tester to apply pressure perpendicular to the laminations. He compared a nanocrystalline alloy (1K107B) with an amorphous alloy (1K101) over a range of frequencies and pressures. He incorporated this stress into a dynamic Jiles–Atherton hysteresis model and checked its predictions against measured hysteresis loops and core losses.

At a peak flux density of **0.8 T** and a frequency of **15 kHz**, the measured loss in the 1K107B sample rose from **45.22 W/kg** with no applied pressure to **114.71 W/kg** at **20 MPa**. The model predicted **114.46 W/kg** under load. With frequency and flux density held constant, compression alone more than doubled the loss in this sample.
