On October 27, 2023, Changgeng Zhang of Hebei University of Technology gave an invited tutorial at Tianjin University titled *Magnetic Measurements of Silicon Steel Sheets in Both Macroscopic and Mesoscopic Scales*. He discussed how to choose measurements that reflect the magnetic conditions a sheet experiences in a transformer, motor or electromagnet.

{% include news-photos.html group="asemd_2023_presentation" %}

## From sheet measurements to magnetic domains

Zhang began with the Epstein frame and the single-sheet tester (SST), which measure a sheet’s response to one-dimensional alternating magnetisation. Where the flux rotates within a lamination, two-dimensional measurements can improve estimates of core loss. At burrs, butt joints or overlapping corners, flux can also cross between laminations. Three-dimensional measurements account for this out-of-plane component.

At the mesoscopic scale, magnetic domain images help explain the response measured across a whole sheet. Zhang compared the Bitter method, which uses magnetic particles to reveal domain walls, with techniques that image domains using light, electrons or a magnetic probe. Together, these measurements inform the design and simulation of magnetic cores.

## About ASEMD

The IEEE International Conference on Applied Superconductivity and Electromagnetic Devices brings together researchers working on superconducting materials and power devices, electrical machines and magnets. Tianjin University and the IEEE Beijing Section co-hosted the 2023 meeting on October 27–29. More than 250 participants attended. Other tutorials included superconducting energy storage and rotational magnetisation.
